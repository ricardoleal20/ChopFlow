Transparency

What these numbers mean

Context matters. We are comparing systems built for different jobs, and every limitation is reported honestly.

Temporal is a durable workflow engine

It persists every step — a heavier category by design. Its numbers are context for what durability costs, not a verdict on ChopFlow.

Ray targets distributed compute

Ray is closer to Dask or Spark than to a task queue. Its throughput includes object-store and cross-actor scheduling overhead — apples-to-pears, included for perspective.

Celery and Ray did not finish at 1M

Celery and Ray 1M runs did not complete within the time budget. They are reported as DNF — not omitted, not estimated, not faked.

  • Metrics are end-to-end: enqueue to completion, including queue wait, serialization, and acknowledgement.
  • Drain timing uses a single ready-count poll per run, not per-task overhead.
  • Latency percentiles are sampled from the first and last 1,000 tasks of each run.
  • All completed runs reported zero failures.
  • Full harness and reproduction steps are in bench/compare/README.md.
Coverage

Why not X?

RQ and Dramatik sit in the same Python/Redis bucket as Celery, so adding them would be redundant rather than informative. Sidekiq, Asynq, and Hatchet require Ruby or Go runtimes that are outside this benchmark’s scope.

The comparison harness is open and documented in bench/compare/README.md. If you want to see another system here, that file is the place to add it.
